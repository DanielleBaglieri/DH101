## Process Notes
Tools, Algorithms, or Systems: 

Based on the limited research I did, this database uses its own specialized software; specifically, the English Corpora interface is used most. They also rely on specialized data applications and analysis of massive text collections. Other than that, the two platforms involved are Google Books and Google Books Ngram. The facinating part of all of this is the fact that there is no AI usage in the creation of this project. It is all within an algorithm that the wedsite is built on. This algorithm sorts through historical documents and the internet looking for keywords that people may be searching for. The choices that are made in this website can shape the meaning by allowing the user to have a free range of choices. The data has little to no room for interpretation. One limitation that I am seeing in this website is the user is not able to look at data that is past the year 2022 due to the fact that it has yet to be updated. A way for this to be altered is to potentially have a running algorithm that updates the data every month 2 months. This can be helpful because with the constant acceleration of computer science and AI, newer models can fix any potential errors made within the data.


## Reflection
For me, the “making” portion of the Google Books Ngram Viewer centers on building the physical database and website that sort through historical data and create the graph that users can then analyze and use in their own projects or research. Making the website is mostly done by humans, with some collaboration with machines. There are a few things that can change the outcome of what is being created. The main thing that I am thinking of as a psychology major is human bias. When we go into something and expect a certain outcome, our human intuition tries to try and find information that aligns with our predicted outcome. A machine doesn’t have these instincts. This means that the data could be more accurate regardless of what you predict will appear. Another thing is just human error; people make small mistakes when doing work, but machines limit these errors because they are not programmed to make them.
