## Reflection
In the image, I wanted to show students' relentless trust in AI models- the idea that they must know everything because they have access to all the resources. However, in reality, AI tends to make up a lot of information when trying to be agreeable to the user. I have been able to test this idea by making up a chapter in a book that did not exist (Ex: chapter 56 in Pride and Prejudice; this doesn’t exist, but it made up information to agree with me). This can be common, especially in English courses, due to the fact that many books are published by multiple publishing companies and there are slight differences in each copy. For this reason, I made my comic center around a student working on an English essay. I used the comic to show my story by using multiple different panels to show the beginning, middle, and end of the story I am portraying: a student struggling with an essay, they use AI to write their essay, and without looking at the result, they turn in the product. This then results in them getting a low grade because the information in the essay was not accurate to the book that she was reading. This project helped me realize that AI is not 100% accurate. You can even see this in the images themselves: the essay is about The Great Gatsby, but the book that is shown in the second image is not related to The Great Gatsby. I think that we have begun to understand this but unfortunately many of us are conditioned to trust it because why wouldn’t it be correct; it has all the information it may need available to it and using our ideas it can improve it. We have to understand that even though it has this, its job is to agree with the user and we cannot alter or change that. In my eyes, I mainly see AI as a tool or collaborator in storytelling. I think that we have the ability to go communicate with the AI without risking a false narrative. When this happens we have the ability to allow our own ideas come through under the data that is provided. This is key because when our creative ideas are diminished in data, it becomes bland and generic.
