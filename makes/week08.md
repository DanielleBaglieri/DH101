## Process Notes
What does your network reveal about power?

- One thing I noticed is that the people critiquing AI companies and AI progress in general are typically highly educated in technology and computer science. This means that these people went to high-level schools and have connections within the tech industry. While it is important to have this perspective, we lose the voices of people who may not have had these opportunities but are still directly impacted by AI and its destruction. We can turn to people who live near these data centers, where they have noise pollution, internet strain, and water loss.

Who is visible? Who is invisible?

- We get to see people in high control/authority within the AI world, for example, CEOs and highly rated authors. You can see the central companies that are at the center of this AI debate. While all of these things are important, it is clear that this map is lacking many voices. As said above, the voices of people who are more directly affected are hidden because they may not have the same credentials that other people that are seen have. For example, Emily Bender got her PhD from the Linguistics Department at Stanford University, and she joined the HPSG and LinGO projects at CSLI. These are very high level credentials that would make her a trusted and reliable author.

How would a different network structure tell a different story?

- If you look at the progress of your information, you might be able to see how our data is filtered by people who may lack the opportunities listed above and they are then being abused. It would be easier to see how our data can be used for corrupt reasons that keep certain people in control and others limited.
