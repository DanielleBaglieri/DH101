## Research
What sources did you use?

- When looking up the locations, I struggled to find direct addresses. I was usually given the area a center was in or planned to be. I found a few sources that showed me all addresses and whether they were in use, planned, or discontinued. I was also able to find news reports that discussed the data review centers in Africa and the inhumane conditions that happened, which was helpful when looking at the locations and the area surrounding them. 

What was hardest to find? 

- For Anthropic, it was very difficult to find the locations of data centers and media filtering. I had to really dig into many different websites just to find the area surrounding a potential center. I could only find two locations for media filtering, and they were in populated areas or in conjunction with a regular data center instead of a purely filtering center. 

What surprised you?

- I thought that finding the locations of these centers would be so much easier than it was. I figured that these companies would want to be as clear as possible due to the fact that AI is such a controversial topic. This was the opposite of what I found: there was almost no information on the internet about these locations.

## Artistic Statement
What argument does your map make?

- My map shows how much information is kept hidden from the public. With only 4 international spaces shown on the internet, one can expect that there is stuff that we do not know about. These companies are worldwide and used that way too; how is it possible that there are only 5 spaces internationally that provide data and information? There is a lot of lost information regarding Content Moderation & Data Labeling Labor in Anthropic. This would connect to the controversy surrounding the issues with the military connection and AI safety incidents.

What would be different if you mapped something else?

- I think that maybe choosing a different company there could have provided more information about the locations of different centers. I think this because I found a lot more information when looking at OpenAI. I wonder if the hidden information is because of all the news that has been going on regarding their technical issues, and CEOs want to keep what they can hidden. I think if I mapped a different company, I would most likely have more luck with finding addresses. 

How does this connect to Data Feminism's ideas about power and visibility?

- We can see how the intersectionality of oppression directly impacts all aspects of the world. When looking at Content Moderation & Data Labeling Labor, you can see how socio-economic status also impacts the abuse that people endure at these data plants. Trigger warning (language of sexual assault): From Time Magazine, they found that at Samasource they had people going through flagged content while getting little pay; one worker “tasked with reading and labeling text for OpenAI told TIME he suffered from recurring visions after reading a graphic description of a man having sex with a dog in the presence of a young child.” (Time Magazine). This is just the tip of the iceberg; since then, OpenAI has said they discontinued this relationship, but there is so much hidden within these companies. One can see how certain companies care about the money that they get from these connections. When they have connections with areas of the world that are known for poverty and low economic status, companies are able to do next to nothing to have their busy work done for them while CEOs and owners get more and more money from users.
